Why "GO for the tool, NARROW for the business," not a clean yes

Testing an idea means walking a chain: does the market exist → does the right kind of buyer exist → will they feel the value → do the economics work → can you reach them. For you as the customer, every link already holds — you exist, you have the Job, you'll feel the value (a cited number beats an uncited one when your own money is on it), the cost is trivial, and "reach" is just opening your own app. That's about as close to a sure thing as product work gets.

For other traders as customers, the chain breaks one link earlier than you'd expect: not at "does verified information matter" (it clearly does, the whole industry has an accuracy problem) but at "will this specific segment pay extra, today, for a cited version of something they already get free or cheap." Our competitor-review research found trust failures are treated as serious when they happen, but nobody in the reviews we found is shopping for "citations" — they're shopping for speed, breadth, low ads, and low price. full chain walk-through ▸

The buyer, in a bit more detail

If you do pursue a business, the strongest early buyer isn't the retail trader in your current design — it's a small professional or semi-professional who gets punished publicly and specifically for publishing a wrong number: a SEBI-registered RIA/RA running a daily client note, or a finance-newsletter/Telegram-channel writer with subscribers. They already have the Job ("never publish a retraction"), they already have budget (they charge their own clients or run ads/subscriptions), and — this is the key asset-fit point — your verification pipeline is generic infrastructure, not a finance-news feature, so it plugs into their workflow as-is. the full segment map ▸

The other candidate segments (retail F&O traders, SIP/mutual-fund retail investors, compliance officers at NBFCs) are all plausible later moves, but each is either lower-margin, needs a B2B sales motion you don't have, or is priced by a market that doesn't yet value the "cited" part enough to switch. segment-by-segment detail ▸

The edge, in plain terms

Every competitor forces a trade-off you don't: Bloomberg/LSEG give you proof but no personal synthesis, and cost more than a personal budget allows; Moneycontrol/Zerodha Pulse/StockEdge give you fast synthesis but no proof a number is right; Perplexity Finance gets close (citations, live prices) but isn't scheduled to your trading day and isn't F&O-specific. Nobody currently sells "both, on a schedule." That gap is real and structurally durable — but our research found it's an unclaimed positioning, not a proven demand: high satisfaction scores across Sensibull (4.78★), TradingView (4.76★) and StockEdge (4.69★) show today's uncited synthesis is tolerated just fine by most users, most of the time. the criteria-by-competitor matrix ▸
